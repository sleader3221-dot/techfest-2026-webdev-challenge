The problem

The fastest-|growing waste|stream

Every year we buy more electronics and keep them for less time. When they stop working, or simply get replaced, most never reach a proper recycler. They're stored and forgotten, thrown in the general bin, or sold to informal scrap dealers who burn and acid-strip them for a few grams of metal.

    Repair|Before|Recycle

    ReCircuit follows the waste hierarchy. The best option is always the one that keeps a device in use longest. Most of a smartphone's lifetime carbon footprint comes from manufacturing it, so keeping it going an extra year or two often does more good than recycling it perfectly.

    What’s inside

    What’s inside|your old phone

    Scroll to take one apart. Every layer holds materials that were mined, shipped and refined, and most of them can be recovered.

    1. 01Screen & glassIndium · Silicon · Glass

      Indium tin oxide makes the touchscreen work. Indium is scarce, and comes mostly as a by-product of zinc mining.

    2. 02Logic boardGold · Silver · Copper · Palladium

      A tonne of old phones holds far more gold than a tonne of gold ore. Certified recyclers recover it without burning.

    3. 03BatteryCobalt · Lithium · Nickel

      Cobalt mining is linked to unsafe work, including child labour. A swollen battery is also a fire risk, so it never goes in the bin.

    4. 04Frame, camera & speakersAluminium · Steel · Neodymium

      Recycled aluminium needs about 95% less energy than new. The tiny magnets use rare-earth metals.

    One phone holds roughly 0.034 g gold, 0.34 g silver, 15 g copper and 6 g cobalt. Indicative figures.
